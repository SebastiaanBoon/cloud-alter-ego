# Writing style: outreach emails

Fill in the placeholders once. Every correction you give on a draft goes into "Do not" below, so the
next round starts better.

## Structure, in this order

1. **Subject:** `Website <Business name>`
2. **Greeting:** `Hi <first name>,` when the contact person is known, otherwise a neutral greeting.
3. **Who:** one sentence: `I am <YOUR NAME> from <YOUR TOWN>. With <YOUR BUSINESS> I build websites for businesses in the area.`
4. **The specific part for this business**, two or three short sentences: what would be on their
   website and what a visitor could do there (see the menu and opening hours, book, request a quote,
   call). Only what follows from the research.
5. **Price:** `A website for this starts at <PRICE>.` Optionally one more sentence when the business
   needs more, such as online payments. Then always: `The final price depends on your wishes.`
6. **Maintenance:** `<WHAT IS INCLUDED AND FROM WHAT PRICE PER MONTH>. The website and domain stay yours.`
7. **Invitation:** `Planning to work on your website? I am happy to drop by, no strings attached.`
8. **Opt-out:** `Not interested? Just let me know and I will not email you again.`
9. **Signature:** name, phone, business name, website, address, company registration number.

Length: around 120 words without the signature, 150 at most. Use one form of address consistently.

## Do not

- **Do not open with the business name.** It reads as generated.
- **No AI slop.** No colon tricks, no lists of three, no slogans, no empty sentences.
- **No jargon.** No words like templates, database, SEO or integrations.
- **No compliments or verdicts** that do not follow from the research. Do not say their site is outdated.
- **No promises** about search rankings or results.
- **No em dashes.**
- **Do not downplay the work:** no "simple", "not complicated" or "easy".
- **No names of other clients**, not even as a reference.
- **No long emails.** Long drafts read as mass mail.
- **Plain text.** No Markdown or HTML in the email.
